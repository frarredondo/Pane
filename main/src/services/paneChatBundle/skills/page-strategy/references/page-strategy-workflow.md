# Page strategy workflow

Use this reference to turn a page idea into a brief.

## Brief structure

1. Page thesis: one sentence on why this page should exist.
2. Target query: primary query, secondary queries, and query class.
3. Searcher job: what the reader came to accomplish.
4. Page type: how-to, comparison, alternatives, template, landing page,
   glossary, guide, checklist, or tool page.
5. Success criteria: what the reader can decide or do after reading.
6. Product role: lead, contextual, related, light mention, or excluded.
7. Proof burden: what the page must show to be trusted.
8. Time to value: where the first real answer must appear.
9. Structure: a section-by-section outline.
10. Differentiation: what this page does better than the current winners.
11. Exclusions: what to leave out because it would distort intent or add
    noise.
12. Review risks: what `page-review` should check after drafting.

## Decision rules

- When the query expects a decision, give criteria before selling.
- When the query expects examples or templates, show usable examples before
  context.
- When the product falls outside the native answer, keep it out of the page's
  frame.
- When the expected answer set excludes the product, the product may appear
  only as a related workflow or as a CTA after value.
- When a comparison or listicle lacks firsthand proof, recommend a narrower
  scope or a research step before writing.

## Strategy questions

- What would make a searcher stop searching?
- What would feel like bait?
- What should appear before the first CTA?
- Which claims need screenshots, data, tests, quotes, or methodology?
- Would the page still be useful with the product removed?
- Does the outline answer the query, or route it into the funnel?
